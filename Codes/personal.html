My report on the richest man in babylon, chapters one and five.

The Richest Man in Babylon chapter 3 follows a man called Arkad who is said to be the richest man in his country. Babylon was not a small country so to be the richest Arkad would have to be wise and adapt a certain lifestyle to hold this title. One day, after the king came back from a battle, his chancellor brought his attention to the financial status of most people in the country. The king realized the problem and invited Arkad to give a talk to some of the citizens to help them get wealthy as well. Arkad presented seven cures/laws to them which were all good, but I specifically took lessons two.

The first cure "Start thy purse to fattening" was one of the lessons I resonated with the most It speaks about setting aside 10% of the money made for saving and 90% for spending. This rule is important as it would help me to not spend more money than I am making and continuously build my saving. I have had a continuous problem of spending and then wondering how my bank account is going down. This way I will be able to budget out 90%, spend it without having to worry about my overall savings going down.

The fourth cure "Guard thy treasures from loss" was the second lesson that I took something from. It is about doing research to ensure that you only invest your capital where your money is safe. Recently I have been thinking about investing money into an index fund. After research, I realized that with more risk, I would be able to pick specific stocks and return way more than the amount promised by the index fund. This chapter convinced me that going on the safer route is really the most beneficial one. Although returns are higher in the next one, they also can also be significantly lower as well which would lose me money.

In chapter 5 titled "The Five Laws of Gold" it starts off with Kalabab asking a group of people if they would rather a heavy bag with gold or a clay tablet carved with wisdom. The group answered with the bag of gold and Kalabab replied to the multitude with several remarks speaking about the bad decision they made. He concluded by saying "Gold is reserved for those who know its laws and abide by them." Kalabab was seemingly a servant of Arkad's child, Nomasir and knew several stories about him. In this chapter he speaks about when Nomasir was given a tablet with wisdom, a bag of gold and 10 years to prove to his father he was worthy of inheriting his wealth. The bag of gold was promptly stolen from him as soon as he exited Babylon but with the wisdom given to him by his father, he was able to go back to Babylon 10 years later and prove to his father that he was ready for his inheritance.

The two chapters I read from the book provided me with great advice on how to make money and keep making money the right way. My biggest take way from this book is the process of building a fortune is not something you do quickly. It is something that takes years of compounding money in consistent, smart and efficient ways. Approaching all the money you get with such mindset is what separates the wealthy from the indigent. It's mostly the mindset that allows rich people to get rich and remain rich. As Kalabab said, " Gold is reserved for those who know its laws and abide by them".
